# HNH Workforce report (HR dashboard, Report Server)

A Python generator writes the HR dashboard as a Power BI project (PBIP): `HNH Workforce.pbip`, with the folders `HNH Workforce.Report/` (pages, visuals, theme, logo) and `HNH Workforce.SemanticModel/` (only the live connection to the `HR Dashboard` perspective of `HNH_Analytics`). Design: `docs/superpowers/specs/2026-11-02-hr-dashboard-design.md`; plan: `docs/superpowers/plans/2026-11-02-hr-dashboard.md`.

To put the dashboard live, follow the [deployment checklist](#deployment-checklist) below, in order.

The generator reuses the Balanced Scorecard generator's modules in `powerbi_tmdl/hnh_bsc_report/` (`frame`, `model`, `pbir`, `fields`, `pbir_check`, and its `pages.py` helpers loaded as `bsc_pages`); nothing is copied. The theme and the logo are the BSC's files. The report has no custom visual, no bookmarks, no visual groups and no report-level measures: every measure is in the model.

Code:

- `hr_frame.py`: `HR_FRAME` (title "Workforce" / "Hayat Healthcare", tabs Home, Story, Scorecard, Details, Data quality, Guide, and the How to use and Definitions buttons), `HR_REPORT` (name, perspective, theme, logo), `page_frame(tab, bar)`, the filter bar (`filters()`: Branch (the hospitals and Head Office) and Month, Month saved on "Last closed month"; `sub_tabs(current)`: the six detail pages), `kpi_tile(code, x, y, w, h)`, and the KPI list read from `scripts/hr_config/hr_config_seed.sql` (`seed_kpis()`, `home_tile_codes()`, `kpi_label()`).
- `pages_home.py`: Home. `pages_story.py`: Story. `pages_scorecard.py`: Scorecard and KPI detail.
- `pages_detail.py`: the six detail pages (Headcount & mix, Movements & turnover, Payroll & overtime, Absence & leave, Recruitment, Productivity) and the hidden Employee list. `pages.py`: `PAGES` (in order) and `REPORT_FILTERS` (this year and last year).

## Detail pages and the Employee list

Each detail page has the Details tab current and the six sub-tabs in the filter bar, its own slicers where every visual on it accepts them, then one or more sections: an optional heading, one or two rows of cards for the selected month, and charts in two columns, then three. Since 2026-11-04 (user request) a bar chart is as high as its bars and labels need (`pages_detail.chart_height`: the axis's bar count in `AXIS_COUNTS`, or 15 for a top 15), other charts are 300 px, and the charts fill their columns top-down; every chart shows its data labels (line charts their markers too) and no value axis or gridlines, and bar labels may take 40 % of the width. Cards and KPI tiles carry decorative icons: `icons/hr_icon_*.svg`, registered with the report (`ReportSpec.images`) and drawn by image visuals after the card (`hr_frame.icon`, `CARD_ICONS`, `KPI_ICONS`). Payroll & overtime has two sections: gross pay (what employees are paid: earnings and earnings adjustments) and payroll cost (what the employer pays: gross pay plus the employer charges, `[Employer Charges]` = `[Payroll Cost]` - `[Gross Pay]`), with a waterfall of payroll cost by pay group and a monthly stack of gross pay and employer charges between them; the open-payroll card is the text `[HR Open Payroll Text]` ("None open", never "(Blank)"). Pages taller than 1080 px are written "FitToWidth" (`pbir.page_json`), so they scroll instead of shrinking. Absence by department and payroll by job are not shown (ruling R26: those facts have no department or job). Line charts show the last 12 closed months whatever the Month slicer says (as on Story). The pages include Head Office, as every page ("Head Office" below). The Department slicer (about 270 prefix-free names), Job, Grade and Nationality have the dropdown's search box.

Every model measure returns "... can't be analysed by <table>" when a table its guard rejects is filtered. `pages_detail.blocked_tables` reads those tables from `_Measures.tmdl`: the build stops when a visual's axis, a page slicer or a drill field is one of them, and a click in a chart does not reach a visual whose measures reject the chart's tables (a NoFilter interaction, written by the generator). So Payroll has only a Department slicer (`Payroll Cost per FTE` rejects Pay Category), Absence & leave, Recruitment and Productivity have none.

Pay figures (Payroll, Productivity, the leave figures on Absence & leave) have an `[HR Pay Notice]` card over them: "Requires pay access" without pay access, and `""` (an empty text) with it, so the card shows nothing. The notice cards have no filter: a card whose filter leaves no row prints "(Blank)", which is what pay users saw over the charts and cards until 2026-11-03. Every note card works that way (`[HR KPI Pay Notice]` on KPI detail, `[HR Vacancy Note]` on the vacancy charts on Recruitment and Story). On a chart or table the note sits in the visual's title strip, right-aligned in 9 pt (ruling R31, `hr_frame.title_notice`): laid over the plot area, even an empty card took the hover and the clicks, so tooltips and cross-highlighting did not work there. A note over a run of pay cards stays centred over the cards. A chart title must leave room for its note (the build stops otherwise), which is why two titles are short: "Overtime share, last 12 closed months" and "Payroll cost per visit (SAR)". Fusion leaver figures are labelled "in Fusion"; the headline turnover is `[BSC Turnover]`.

Departments and positions (ruling R30): charts and the Department slicer use the prefix-free `'HR Department'[Department Base Name]` and `'Position'[Position Base Name]` ("Nursing Department", "Staff Nurse"), so a name shared by the hospitals is one bar; the per-hospital department ("RBW Nursing Department") is only on the Employee list. The model's "Unknown" (no department, location, job or staff recorded) is the same label in every report; the Guide explains it.

FTE equals headcount today (Fusion FTE values are 1; a zero FTE is replaced by 1), so the Headcount page's FTE and average cards show whole numbers (the card's decimal places; the measures keep their formats). Recruitment: no budgeted FTE is loaded yet, so the vacancy rate is blank; its cards (Recruitment and Home's area card) are `[HR Vacancy Rate Text]` ("No budgeted FTE") and the vacancy charts carry `[HR Vacancy Note]`. Both turn into the rate and an empty note once `BSC Budgeted FTE` has the year's rows.

The Employee list is reached by drill-through on a branch or a (prefix-free) department, with "keep all filters" off (a source visual's own filters, such as 'HR KPI' or 'Pay Category', would turn `[Headcount]` into its guard message). The month and the Department, Job, Grade, Nationality and Worker type selections come from its slicers, synced with the detail pages'. Its one table is filtered to `[Headcount]` not blank, so branch security applies through the fact. It groups by Employee columns only (worker number, person number, hire date, status; the person number keeps people without a worker number apart); the Oasis staff ID, branch, department, job and grade are the measures `[HR Employee Staff ID]`, `[HR Employee Hospital]`, `[HR Employee Department]`, `[HR Employee Job]` and `[HR Employee Grade]`, read through the month's headcount row. A table grouped by the columns of six dimensions ran for minutes on the test slot (over 160 s of CPU; Desktop showed an empty table); grouped by employee the same 4,314 people return in about 2 s. The Fusion name is the measure `[HR Employee Name]` (blank without pay access), never an 'Employee Details' column: a table grouped by a column with no visible rows has no rows, so the column would empty the list for readers without pay access. `[Headcount]` is a column too, because a table drops the rows where all its measures are blank.
- `build.py`: the CLI.

Test with `python -m pytest powerbi_tmdl/hnh_hr_report/tests -q`. Both report folders have a `pages.py` and a `build.py`; the HR tests load this folder's `pages.py` as `hr_pages` so that `python -m pytest powerbi_tmdl` runs both suites in one session.

## Build the project

Run from the repository root:

`python powerbi_tmdl/hnh_hr_report/build.py`

The arguments are the BSC `build.py`'s. The defaults are `--database HNH_Analytics_Test` and `--server HNHANALYTICSSRV\REPORTSERVERDB`; `--out` is the folder for the project and defaults to this folder. Only the project is built (`--format pbip`); `--pbiviz` is not used. The command stops if a field is not in the `HR Dashboard` perspective. It then prints `report schemas: passed` and `Desktop project reader: opened`, and exits 1 on any problem. Commit the regenerated project.

The server reads only the legacy package: write it with `powerbi_tmdl/hnh_bsc_report/pbir_to_pbix.py` as `powerbi_tmdl/README.md` describes (section "HNH Workforce (HR dashboard) route": the commands, the checks on first open, and the switch to production).

`python powerbi_tmdl/hnh_bsc_report/pbir_to_pbix.py "powerbi_tmdl/hnh_hr_report/HNH Workforce.pbip" --out "C:\HNH\report\HNH Workforce.pbix"`

The switch to production is `build.py --database HNH_Analytics` after the model is deployed to production; then convert again.

## KPI tiles and status

A KPI tile shows `[HR KPI Actual Text]` (the formatted actual; "Requires pay access" for a pay KPI without pay access) under the KPI's short label, `[HR KPI Target Text]` (with "(placeholder)" while the target is one), and `[HR KPI Status Text]` (shape and word) in the colour of `[HR KPI Status Colour]`, set as the card's value colour by conditional formatting (field value). Home's tiles are the seed's `is_home_tile` KPIs in `display_order`, read from `scripts/hr_config/hr_config_seed.sql` when the report is built: a change to `is_home_tile` or `short_label` needs the seed file changed and the report rebuilt (`docs/hr_config.md`). Pages that show these measures carry only the Branch and Month slicers, and on Scorecard and KPI detail the Period slicer. Home's Payroll and Productivity area cards use the pay-aware `[HR Payroll Cost Text]` and `[HR Visits per FTE Text]`.

The Scorecard table shows, per KPI, the status, actual, target, variance (`[HR KPI Variance Text]`, actual minus target in the KPI's format) and watch value (`[HR KPI Watch Text]`). Every one of them is a text measure that says "Requires pay access" for a pay KPI to a reader without pay access: a pay KPI's target is a pay level, so `[HR KPI Target]`, `[HR KPI Watch]` and `[HR KPI Variance]` are blank for that reader too (final review C1). KPI detail's chart notice is `[HR KPI Pay Notice]`, shown only for a pay KPI. Definitions shows the target and the watch value. The Scorecard and Definitions tables have fixed column widths that fill the table (`hr_frame.fixed_table`: autosize off, wrapped text, no total row) and labelled columns (Status, Actual, Target, Variance, Watch at). The drill-through field of KPI detail is `'HR KPI'[KPI Name]` (unique, like the code), a column of both the table and the matrix, so the table has no KPI Code column and a matrix row drills too. Opened without a drill-through, KPI detail's heading and definition (`[HR KPI Detail Title]`, `[HR KPI Definition Text]`) say how to pick a KPI.

Revenue per FTE is scored as the BSC scores LRN_REVENUE_PER_FTE: same actual, same target, same status for the same month and hospitals (check `revenue_per_fte_target_status_equal_bsc_one_month_2026_10`). It reads At risk for October 2026 because the GL revenue of that month is not posted yet (SAR 2.5M of a SAR 176M budget; September is partly posted), not because of a period mismatch: the BSC report shows the same for that month. Its status is the BSC's, which is worked out for the hospitals only, while the actual and target shown are the HR measures over every selected branch, Head Office included. They agree today because the GL and `Workforce Monthly` have no Head Office rows. Should Head Office GL or workforce rows ever arrive, the status could disagree with the all-branch actual and target shown next to it (it would still describe the hospitals); re-check `revenue_per_fte_target_status_equal_bsc_one_month_2026_10` then. The revenue KPIs keep months whose GL is still open (user decision 2026-11-04): the figure is what is posted so far, as in the BSC.

The Month slicer is single-select on `'Date'[Month Pick]` and saved on "Last closed month": the model gives that label to the previous month each day, so every page opens on the last closed month without a rebuild. Scorecard and KPI detail also have Period (single-select on `'Time Calculation'`, saved on Current; Current, QTD, YTD), synced between those two pages: under YTD the KPIs read January to the selected month, a flow KPI's annual target pro-rated by the months and a month-end KPI at the month end. KPI detail's 24-month line ignores Period. Story stays on one month (R18).

## Head Office (user decision 2026-11-03)

Head Office (branch 100) is in everything the report shows: the Branch slicer (labelled Branch: the eight hospitals and Head Office), every page, every KPI and its target, placeholders, Story, Productivity and Data quality. This supersedes ruling R14 (HR KPI actual and target over hospitals only) and final review I1 (hospitals-only page filters). Every data page carries one page filter, `'Branch'[Branch Key]` in 1-8 and 100 (`hr_frame.branches()`): it keeps out only the Branch dimension's Group member (0), a synthetic all-hospitals row with no HR data, which a text measure such as `[HR KPI Status Text]` would otherwise give a "– No data" column. The filter is locked in view mode (`isLockedInViewMode`, as the BSC's report-level filter; `hr_frame.lock_branch_filters`, applied by `build.py` after writing the project, because the shared generator writes page filters unlocked), so a reader cannot clear it from the filter pane. The HR measures themselves have no branch restriction (dates up to today stay). Branch row security is unchanged: `HNH Readers` filters Branch by `'User Access'[Branch Key]`, and `sec_user_access` grants branch 100 to administrators only (and to a source row that names it), so other readers neither see Head Office in the slicer nor in any figure.

The BSC report is not changed: it stays hospitals only. For the KPIs the two reports share, the HR figure reads the BSC measure without the hospital filter, so at network level it could differ from the BSC report. Once Head Office's requisitions are built (ruling R32), vacancy rate and time to fill do; the facts behind the others hold no Head Office rows (read-only DAX on HNH_Analytics_Test, Year 2026 and September 2026; check `bsc_shared_kpis_network_equals_hospitals_2026_09`):

## Deployment checklist

In this order. A step marked **user's go-ahead** changes the production server or live data (`CLAUDE.md`): ask at the time, every time. The other steps are read-only.

If the config tables do not exist yet, create them with `scripts\hr_config\apply_hr_config.ps1` (`-DryRun` first; the real run is a ClickHouse write, its own go-ahead). Copy the models as `docs/receiving_project_config.md` ("HR dashboard") lists them, then:
   `dbt build --select stg_ref__hr_*+ fact_hr_dq_record+ dim_date+ hnh_dim_position+ hnh_dim_hr_department+ hnh_fact_recruiting_requisition+` (the last one: Head Office's requisitions, ruling R32; `stg_ref__hr_*+` also rebuilds `fact_hr_target_monthly`, whose grid now includes Head Office, branch 100; the last two: the prefix-free `position_base_name` and the Unknown department's base name, ruling R30; `'Position'[Position Base Name]` fails to process without them)
2. **Regenerate the view contracts** (read-only): `python scripts/gen_view_contracts.py`. It needs `clickhouse_connect`; where it is missing, use the ODBC equivalent through DSN `HNH_Gold` (ruling R10: the same `describe` per `gold.ssas_*` view; installing the package is a software install, its own go-ahead). Confirm that the only change is the 4 HR views, that `month_pick String` is the last column of `ssas_dim_date` (`python -m pytest ssas/tools/test_hr_perspective.py`) and that `position_base_name Nullable(String)` follows `position_name` in `ssas_dim_position` (both entries were added by hand).
3. **Model to the test slot** (user's go-ahead): `powershell -ExecutionPolicy Bypass -File ssas\scripts\deploy.ps1 -Stage TestMeasures`, then (read-only) `powershell -ExecutionPolicy Bypass -File ssas\scripts\test.ps1 -Database HNH_Analytics_Test -Stage Security` and the same with `-Stage Measures` (one stage per run: `-File` passes `Security,Measures` as one word). The Security stage logs on as each user of `ssas/tests/security.json` and, with the HR tables deployed, checks `HR pay targets`: for a user without pay access (`EXE_ABH`, `CTL_RBW`) every pay KPI's target, watch value and variance are blank and their texts and `[HR KPI Pay Notice]` say "Requires pay access"; for a pay user (`HRM_CORP`) they never do, and the notice is `""`.    2. `scripts\hr_config\apply_hr_config.ps1 -Update <file>` (user's go-ahead: ClickHouse write).
   3. `dbt build --select stg_ref__hr_*+` in the receiving project (user's go-ahead; the file also re-inserts `map_hr_kpi` rows, so not only `stg_ref__hr_target+`).
   4. `powershell -ExecutionPolicy Bypass -File ssas\scripts\process.ps1 -Database HNH_Analytics_Test -Mode Daily -Force` (user's go-ahead).
   5. `test.ps1 -Database HNH_Analytics_Test -Stage Measures`, then `-Stage Security`, again (read-only): the direction-rule and YTD checks now compare real targets (ruling R12). Commit the update file.
5. **Report.** `python powerbi_tmdl/hnh_hr_report/build.py`, then `pbir_to_pbix.py` and `pbix.check_upload_rules` (`[]`) as `powerbi_tmdl/README.md` ("HNH Workforce (HR dashboard) route") shows. Open the file in Desktop for Report Server and go through that section's first-open checks, as `HRM_CORP` and as `EXE_ABH`. Upload (user's go-ahead).

### The production Date table

Step 1 rebuilds `gold.ssas_dim_date` with one more column, `month_pick`, which the production `HNH_Analytics` model does not have until the HR model is deployed there.

- **How a partition reads its view.** Every partition is a Power Query (M) partition, `Odbc.Query("dsn=HNH_Gold", "select * from gold.<view>")` (`ssas/tools/hnh_tmdl.py` `odbc_expression`; the Date table's in `ssas/HNH_Analytics/tables/Date.tmdl`). Each data column of the table names its source with `sourceColumn`; the engine fills each such column from the result column of that name. A result column that no `sourceColumn` names is not loaded; a `sourceColumn` the result lacks fails the refresh. By that binding the extra `month_pick` is ignored, and the Daily process (`process.ps1` refreshes the Date table DataOnly every day) loads the production Date table as before.
- **Not yet proven on this server.** The production model was last processed on 2026-10-11 (`$SYSTEM.TMSCHEMA_PARTITIONS`: all 528 partitions), and 12 of its tables already read views with columns it does not bind (2026-11-03): Branch (`bsc_category`, `is_hospital`), Payroll (`oasis_payment_amount`, `oasis_deduction_amount`), Claim Lines (`claim_status_key`, `response_benefit_amount`), Invoices (`claim_status_key`), Admissions and Encounters (`is_referral_policy`), the three staff role tables (`is_high_value_specialty`), Clinic Capacity (`bsc_capacity_slots`), Product Category (`is_bsc_ancillary`), Revenue Adjustments (`staff_key`). No production process has run since, so the next one meets extra columns whether or not step 1 runs.
- **If it fails.** `process.ps1` commits the whole refresh in one transaction: a column error rolls it back, the production data stays as it was, and the script (and Dagster) exits 1.
- **Check before the nightly production run** (user's go-ahead): run `powershell -ExecutionPolicy Bypass -File ssas\scripts\process.ps1 -Database HNH_Analytics -Mode Daily -Force` once by hand and read its log in `ssas/logs` (`Daily processing of HNH_Analytics committed`). It covers the Date table and the 12 tables above, and changes nothing if it fails. If it fails on a column, deploy the current model to production (`deploy.ps1 -Stage Promote`, which binds `month_pick` and the other columns) before the nightly run, or process the test slot's Date table under pre-HR metadata to confirm the cause.
